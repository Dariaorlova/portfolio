CONTEXT & RESEARCH OBJECTIVES

The company was developing new product and marketing strategies. To do so, both marketing and product teams required a deep understanding of users' needs, behaviors, and pain points. The program evolved as new business questions emerged, with each research stage addressing a specific question.

Evolving research objectives:

Understand the audience

Identify user needs, behaviors, and pain points.

Identify and validate user segments

Understand whether stable user types exist and how their needs evolve with experience.

Prioritize product opportunities

Prioritize user tasks and assess satisfaction with existing solutions.

Support business decisions

Create actionable buyer personas for marketing and study high-value users to inform the development of a premium experience.

Strategy & approach

Instead of starting straight with interviews, I proposed reviewing existing research first. This helped identify knowledge gaps, avoid duplicating previous work, and establish a foundation for the research program.

I designed and coordinated an international research program, selecting methods for each research question, collaborating with product analytics, and managing external research partners. Each stage of the program was designed to answer specific questions and validate hypotheses, while also generating new insights that informed the next stages of research.

01. Understanding the audience
01 / Research Audit
I reviewed 41 previous studies, including interviews, surveys, and usability tests, to consolidate existing knowledge and formulate initial hypotheses. The resulting knowledge base provided a foundation for the research program.
02 / Behavioral Analytics
Together with a data analyst, we identified behavioral patterns and defined sampling criteria for the qualitative research.
03 / Research Pilot
We conducted a pilot study to test initial hypotheses and refine the interview guide before launching international fieldwork.
04 / International Interviews
I coordinated native-speaking research moderators conducting interviews across eight countries. The goal was to explore user motivations, test hypotheses, and develop a preliminary user typology.
05 / Quantitative Validation
I designed and conducted a quantitative survey to assess the qualitative findings and test whether the proposed user segments could be reliably identified.
06 / Opportunity Score
We assessed the importance of user tasks and satisfaction with existing solutions to identify priorities and potential product opportunities.
02. Enabling market expansion
07 / Buyer Personas
As new business questions emerged, I worked with stakeholders to translate audience insights into research objectives linked to marketing decisions. To do this, I developed actionable buyer personas for target markets, combining user motivations, behaviors, financial goals, and barriers to inform communication strategies, marketing campaigns, and market expansion.
03. Understanding high-value users

A separate research stream explored the needs, behaviors, and expectations of high-value users to support the development of a premium experience.

08 / First Research Wave
I investigated high-value users across four markets, exploring their motivations, trading behaviors, needs, and expectations. The findings generated new hypotheses and highlighted limitations in the initial audience definition.
09 / Follow-up Research
Using refined audience criteria, we conducted a follow-up study to investigate the hypotheses from the first wave, focusing on trading activity, trust, personal support, and premium service expectations.
What We Achieved

Customer understanding: this research helped the team to understand users' key needs, behaviors, and pain points. We managed to prioritize user tasks and identify differences across markets in user behavior and needs.

Marketing adoption: The gathered insights informed the product and marketing strategies for expansion into new markets, and helped to improve customer communication journeys and marketing campaigns.

Premium experience: We identified the gap between how business perceived high-value users and their actual behaviors, needs, and circumstances. We learned a lot about high-value users and their unique behaviors, needs, and expectations. This informed the redesign of the status system.

What Didn't Work Out

Segmentation: The qualitative user typology was not reliably validated by the quantitative analysis. We couldn't identify stable, reproducible user segments or operationalize the proposed segmentation.

Opportunity Score: The results helped us identify user priorities. However, ratings were consistently high across most tasks, making it difficult to distinguish clearly underserved needs.

User development: Our goal was to determine the users' development from novice to high-value user. We couldn't establish a clear progression as users' motivations and behaviors overlapped, and the data couldn't confirm actual transitions over time.

Key Takeaway

The key lesson was that a universal user model wasn't always achievable. The research created greater value when we connected specific questions to real product and marketing decisions.
